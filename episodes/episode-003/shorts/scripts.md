## Short 1 · GravityAssistSpeed
**Title:** How to Steal Speed From a Planet (v1: How Can a Planet Speed Up a Spacecraft?)

1. A spacecraft can fly past a planet, fire no engines, and leave faster than it arrived. `C02` `C06`
2. Watch from the planet first. Gravity pulls it in, swings it around, and lets it go. `C03`
3. Measured from the planet, it leaves about as fast as it arrived. Only its direction has changed. `C01` `C05`
4. But the planet is moving around the Sun. Seen from the Sun, that curve is carried along with it, and the spacecraft leaves faster. `C02` `C04`
5. The energy isn't free. It's borrowed from the planet's own orbit, by an almost imperceptible amount. `C08` `C09`
6. Same flyby. Two answers. Both of them right.

## Short 2 · ReachingTheSun
**Title:** How to Fall Into the Sun (v1: How Do You Reach the Sun When Earth Is Moving So Fast?)

1. Falling into the Sun sounds easy. It isn't.
2. Earth is racing sideways around the Sun at about thirty kilometres a second. And so is everything we launch from it. `C13`
3. Aim straight at the Sun, and that sideways speed carries you right past it. `C14`
4. NASA's Parker Solar Probe needed to shed about fifty-three thousand miles per hour of it. `C15`
5. So it flew past Venus seven times. Together, those flybys reduced its orbital energy around the Sun, and its orbit reached closer in. `C31` `C11`
6. Then it fell. On Christmas Eve, twenty twenty-four, it passed closer to the Sun than any spacecraft before it. `C32` `C40`

## Short 3 · VoyagerAlignment
**Title:** How to Fly to Uranus (v1: Why Did Voyager Need a Rare Planetary Alignment?)

1. One spacecraft. Four giant planets. One trip. That only works if the planets are in just the right places. `C22`
2. In the late nineteen-seventies, they drifted into a favourable alignment, one that comes around roughly once every hundred and seventy-five years. `C25`
3. Not a straight line. But it made a journey past all four possible, and Voyager 2 took it. `C25` `C22` `C18`
4. At Jupiter, gravity bent its path toward Saturn. At Saturn, toward Uranus. At Uranus, toward Neptune, which it reached in nineteen eighty-nine. `C20` `C22`
5. Each flyby reshaped its route. Measured from the Sun, together they more than doubled its speed. `C24`
