[on screen: diagram panel; amber Jupiter arrow, cyan arrow tip-to-tail, white resultant]

[on screen: two-pan energy balance; the craft's bar rises]

## Notes for review

1. **Scene 3, "about as fast."** "About" covers an ideal unpowered flyby. NASA states the speeds match exactly in the ideal case (C01), so "exactly" is also defensible. Choose one at review.
2. **Scene 6, "barely changes."** Jupiter's arrow is drawn unchanged. "Barely" keeps the script consistent with Scene 7 (Jupiter gives up a little).
3. **Scene 9, "Voyager 2 took that chance."** The claim behind it is C22 (only Voyager 2 visited all four planets). The script doesn't say NASA designed the launch around this; it only says the alignment allowed it (C25).
4. **Scene 11, miles per hour.** C15 is NASA's imperial figure, while Scene 10 uses km/s. Either keep both units, as NASA gives them, or add a converted km/h figure to the claims list (about 85,000 km/h) before changing it.
5. **Scene 12, the kilometre figure** is a conversion of NASA's 3.8 million miles (C32). The speed is spoken qualitatively ("faster than anything humans have built") and the on-screen label carries the number.
6. **Scene 14 is future tense** and is valid only if the video is published before 18 Nov 2026. If it publishes later, change the line to past tense after NASA confirms (C30). "A day" follows from the definition of a light-day (one way).
7. **Slot fit.** Scene 12 runs about 2 s over its 15 s slot, and Scenes 1, 8 and 14 fill theirs exactly. Scenes 5 and 6 have 6–8 s spare, so moving about 3 s from Scene 6 to Scene 12 keeps the 6:10 total. Retime from the real takes.
8. **Not used:** the optional "12 vs 30 km/s" comparison (C17), BepiColombo, the mass ratio, and any Voyager speed numbers.
9. **Pronunciation check on first listen:** "Io", "Titan", "Parker", "Voyager", and "kilometres" (the en-us voice may prefer "kilometers"; the spelling doesn't change the audio).
