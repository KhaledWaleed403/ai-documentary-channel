## Concept
- **Central idea:** Water heated alone in a very clean cup can pass its boiling point without bubbling, then erupt when disturbed, and two simple habits reduce the risk.
- **Curiosity gap:** How can water be hotter than boiling and not boil?
- **Opening hook (new, written for this Short):** "Water can get hotter than boiling — and still not boil."
- **Arc:** Hook (paradox) → mechanism (boiling needs somewhere to start; bubbles form around tiny impurities) → setup (plain water, clean cup: climbs past boiling while calm) → reveal (a small disturbance and the bubbles burst out at once; can scald) → practical (don't exceed recommended times; add coffee or sugar before heating) → payoff (calm doesn't always mean below boiling).
- **Why it stands alone:** The paradox is stated in the first line and resolved within the Short.
- **How it differs:** Its central idea is nucleation and a safety habit; it shares no explanation with the others.
- **Accuracy guardrails:** No frequency claim ("rare") is made. "Before heating" is stressed because adding powder to already superheated water is itself a trigger (X12, X13). Scratches as nucleation sites aren't claimed.

## Full narration

> Water can get hotter than boiling — and still not boil.
>
> Boiling needs somewhere to start. Bubbles usually form around tiny impurities in the water.
>
> Heat plain water by itself in a very clean cup, and it can climb past boiling while looking completely calm.
>
> Then a small disturbance — picking up the cup, adding a spoonful of coffee — and bubbles burst out all at once. Boiling water can erupt from the cup.
>
> So don't heat water longer than the instructions recommend. And adding instant coffee or sugar before heating greatly reduces the risk.
>
> Calm doesn't always mean below boiling. That water may just be waiting for a reason to boil.
