## Concept
- **Central idea:** History: how one melted candy bar, investigated on purpose, became the microwave oven. Focus on the discovery and the experiments, not the physics of heating.
- **Curiosity gap:** How does a ruined snack turn into a kitchen appliance?
- **Opening hook (new, written for this Short):** "The microwave oven started with a ruined snack."
- **Arc:** Hook (a ruined snack) → setup (Spencer beside a magnetron, the bar has melted) → development (deliberate tests: popcorn pops, egg explodes) → reveal (concentrate the waves in a small space and food heats fast; patent filed Oct 1945) → payoff (every oven still runs on that idea; pressing Start uses the answer to the candy bar's question).
- **Why it stands alone:** Names who, where and when in the first 15 s and explains the magnetron in one clause. Needs no prior knowledge of how microwaves heat.
- **How it differs:** The completed Short and the other new Shorts explain mechanisms or kitchen practice; this is the only historical story. It never explains dielectric heating.
- **Accuracy guardrails:** No dialogue or inner thoughts are given to Spencer, the bar is never named or called chocolate (long-form D01), the egg splatter on a colleague is omitted (L42), and the chamber is generic (L07). "Started with" refers to the microwave *oven*, not cooking with radio energy, which had been tried earlier (X15/L09).

## Full narration

> The microwave oven started with a ruined snack.
>
> In the mid-nineteen-forties, engineer Percy Spencer was working near a magnetron — the tube that makes radar's waves. Then he noticed the candy bar in his pocket had melted.
>
> So he tested food on purpose. Popcorn kernels by the magnetron popped. An egg exploded.
>
> Then the key step: he concentrated the waves in a small space. Food placed there heated fast.
>
> In October, nineteen forty-five, Raytheon filed a patent for cooking with microwaves.
>
> Microwave ovens still work that way — a magnetron filling a metal box with waves. Every time you press Start, you're using the answer to the question a melted candy bar raised.
