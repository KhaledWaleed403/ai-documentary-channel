## Concept
- **Central idea:** Practical safety: why a dish can get hot, the difference between heat from the food and the dish absorbing energy itself, and what "microwave-safe" actually means.
- **Curiosity gap:** If the microwave heats the food, why is the plate scorching?
- **Opening hook (new, written for this Short):** "If a microwave heats the food, why is the plate scorching?"
- **Arc:** Hook (scorching plate) → reason 1 (harmless: hot food heats what it touches; waves pass through the dish) → reason 2 (the dish itself absorbs; most ceramics barely do, some get very hot) → development (the material's name doesn't settle it: material, glaze, metal trim, intended use) → reveal (the label means tested; not melting ≠ passing) → practical check (the water-cup check flags a hot dish; a cool result is a good sign, not a guarantee) → payoff (check the label, follow the maker's instructions; the food should be the hottest thing out).
- **Why it stands alone:** Starts from an everyday observation and defines both causes on screen; no microwave physics needed.
- **How it differs:** The completed Short explains how food heats. This Short is only about containers. It doesn't mention molecules, hot spots or turntables.
- **Accuracy guardrails:** Never says all ceramic is unsafe or all glass or plastic is safe (X02). Says "not melting isn't passing" (X04) and calls the home check "not a guarantee" (X06). The label meaning is stated only for plastic (X03). It points viewers to the maker's instructions (X07).

## Full narration

> If a microwave heats the food, why is the plate scorching?
>
> Sometimes it's the food: hot food heats whatever it touches, even a dish the waves pass through.
>
> But sometimes the dish itself absorbs the energy. Most ceramics barely do — some get very hot on their own.
>
> So ceramic, glass or plastic doesn't settle it. How the dish was made — and what for — does.
>
> That's what a microwave-safe label is for. On plastic, it means the container passed tests to FDA standards. Not melting isn't passing.
>
> No label? Heat the empty dish beside a cup of water for a minute. Dish hot? Keep it out. Dish cool? A good sign — not a guarantee.
>
> Check the label, follow the maker's instructions — and the food should be the hottest thing out.
