# Episode 001 — Script

**Title (working):** How Does a Microwave Actually Heat Food?
**Format:** YouTube Short · 9:16 · 1080×1920 · 30 fps · target 42 s
**Narration:** 101 words, about 2.5 words/s, ending with a ~2 s silent end card
**Status:** draft v3 (plain-language and accuracy review), ready for narration review
**Research:** claim IDs (C##) and source IDs refer to `research/episode-001/claims.md` and `sources.md`.

## Full narration
> Your microwave doesn't heat the air. So what's heating your food?
> Here, this part turns electricity into microwaves — a kind of radio wave.
> They bounce around the metal box and into your food.
> Water molecules have positive and negative ends. The waves tug them back and forth, billions of times a second.
> All that tugging and bumping turns the waves' energy into heat. The waves reach partway in — heat spreads the rest of the way.
> Some spots get stronger waves, so the turntable keeps food moving. Still — give it a stir.
> Waves in, hot food out — and nothing radioactive left behind.

## Segments

### SH01 · 0.0–4.0 s · Hook
- **VO:** "Your microwave doesn't heat the air. So what's heating your food?"
- **On-screen text:** "So what's heating it?"
- **Viewer sees:** Warm-ivory background. A cold bowl of soup (mist-blue tint) slides into a flat, front-on microwave and the door shuts. A small thermometer icon in the oven's air stays low.
- **Transition:** The camera pushes into the side panel, which starts to peel away.
- **Claims:** C01

### SH02 · 4.0–8.5 s · The source
- **VO:** "Here, this part turns electricity into microwaves — a kind of radio wave."
- **On-screen text:** label "magnetron"
- **Viewer sees:** Ink-navy cutaway of the oven with a ghosted ivory outline. A neutral ivory power line pulses into the magnetron block, which is shown as a simple labelled box. A teal wave comes out of it and travels down a short channel into the oven.
- **Transition:** Follow the teal wave out of the channel into the oven space (match on motion).
- **Claims:** C02, C03, C15

### SH03 · 8.5–12.5 s · Bouncing
- **VO:** "They bounce around the metal box and into your food."
- **On-screen text:** none
- **Viewer sees:** Mist-blue side view of the oven. Teal waves bounce off the metal walls, and a few paths cross the bowl. Where they enter the soup they fade gradually as they go in, instead of stopping at the surface.
- **Transition:** Zoom into the soup. A scale bar ticks down from "cm" to "nm".
- **Claims:** C04, C09

### SH04 · 12.5–20.5 s · The tug
- **VO:** "Water molecules have positive and negative ends. The waves tug them back and forth, billions of times a second."
- **On-screen text:** "+" and "−" on one molecule; badge "Schematic · not to scale"
- **Viewer sees:** Ink-navy close-up. A few V-shaped water molecules, one marked + and −. Teal arrows show the wave's pull switching direction. The molecules twist part of the way to follow, lagging and out of sync, never in neat full spins.
- **Transition:** The twisting gets crowded and messy, carrying straight into SH05.
- **Claims:** C05, C06, C07

### SH05 · 20.5–29.5 s · Energy becomes heat
- **VO:** "All that tugging and bumping turns the waves' energy into heat. The waves reach partway in — heat spreads the rest of the way."
- **On-screen text:** small label "dielectric heating" (close-up part only); badge "Schematic · not to scale" stays on while molecules are visible
- **Viewer sees:** The close-up continues while the background warms from navy to ivory. Tugged molecules knock into their neighbors, and the tidy back-and-forth turns into random jiggling. A thermometer icon rises. Pull back and cut to an ivory cross-section of the soup. Teal waves enter and fade within a thick outer zone, and that whole zone warms to orange. It's a soft band with real depth, not a thin skin. Then the centre slowly warms as heat spreads inward.
- **Transition:** The cross-section tilts flat into a top-down view of the oven floor.
- **Claims:** C07, C09, C16

### SH06 · 29.5–36.0 s · Hot spots & the turntable
- **VO:** "Some spots get stronger waves, so the turntable keeps food moving. Still — give it a stir."
- **On-screen text:** "hot" / "cold" tags on two patches of food
- **Viewer sees:** Ink-navy top-down view. Irregular brighter and dimmer teal areas mark stronger and weaker waves. For about 2 s the plate sits still and heats in patches (orange next to mist blue). Then the turntable rotates and the food passes through the brighter and dimmer areas. The patches soften, but a few cooler spots remain. A spoon icon gives one quick stir.
- **Transition:** The turntable slows to a stop and the view cuts to the front of the oven as the door swings open.
- **Claims:** C10, C11

### SH07 · 36.0–42.0 s · Payoff
- **VO:** "Waves in, hot food out — and nothing radioactive left behind."
- **On-screen text:** "Waves in. Hot food out." then the end card "See what makes it work." with the Luminexplain wordmark
- **Viewer sees:** Warm-ivory background. The bowl slides out with soft steam curls. A small teal wave icon turns into a raised thermometer icon. The end card holds about 2 s with no voiceover.
- **Transition:** End.
- **Claims:** C12

## Wording checks
- **Words to avoid:** never say "resonate" (C08), "from the inside" or "from the outside in" (C09), "evenly" or "uniform" (C11), or "radiation" in a way that suggests the food keeps it (C12).
- **Exact numbers:** "Billions of times a second" is correct for ~2.45 GHz (C05). Don't put an exact number or penetration depth on screen (D02).
- **Schematic badge:** "Schematic · not to scale" must be visible whenever molecules are on screen (SH04 and SH05's close-up).
- **Visual metaphors:** heat is shown only as colour and a thermometer icon, never as particles moving between molecules (C07).
- **Water as the example:** the narration names water; fats and sugars also absorb (C16). This is a deliberate simplification.
- **Thick food:** "The waves reach partway in" describes thick food like a bowl of soup (C09). Thin foods can be reached all the way through. The visual shows a thick bowl, so they agree.
