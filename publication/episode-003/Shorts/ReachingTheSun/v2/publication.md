**Description**
```
Falling into the Sun sounds easy. It isn't. Earth races sideways around the Sun at about 30 km/s, and so does everything launched from it: aim straight at the Sun and you swing right past. Parker Solar Probe had to shed about 53,000 mph of that sideways motion, so it flew past Venus seven times. Together, those flybys reduced its orbital energy, and on 24 December 2024 it passed closer to the Sun than any spacecraft before it.

Sources
• NASA Goddard, It's Surprisingly Hard to Go to the Sun: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/13017
• NASA, Parker Solar Probe Makes History With Closest Pass to Sun: https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/heliophysics/nasas-parker-solar-probe-makes-history-with-closest-pass-to-sun/
```
