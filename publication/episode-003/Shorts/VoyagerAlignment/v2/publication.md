One spacecraft, four giant planets, one trip. In the late 1970s the outer planets drifted into a favourable alignment, one that comes around roughly once every 175 years. It wasn't a straight line, but it made a route past all four possible. Voyager 2 took it: gravity at Jupiter, Saturn and Uranus bent its path from one planet to the next, and it reached Neptune in 1989. Measured from the Sun, those flybys more than doubled its speed.
