In 1977, Voyager 2 left Earth without enough speed to escape the Sun. Then it flew past Jupiter, fired no engines, and came out on a path to the edge of the Solar System. Where did that speed come from?

This video explains gravity assists, the trick that sent Voyager 2 past all four giant planets and let Parker Solar Probe pass closer to the Sun than any spacecraft before it. The key is a change of view: seen from the planet, a flyby only turns a spacecraft. Seen from the Sun, that same turn can make it faster, or slower.

Questions this video answers:
• How can flying past a planet make a spacecraft faster without using fuel?
• Where does the extra energy come from, and does the planet slow down?
• Why did Voyager 2 need a favourable alignment that comes around roughly once every 175 years?
• Why is it so hard to reach the Sun?
• How did seven Venus flybys bring Parker Solar Probe to its record-close pass?
