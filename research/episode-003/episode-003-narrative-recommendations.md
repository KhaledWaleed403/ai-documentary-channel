## 2. Key elements

- **Strongest opening:** *Voyager 2's counterfactual orbit* (C23). A dashed ellipse between Earth and Jupiter, "where it should have stayed," dissolves into the real path that bends at Jupiter and heads outward. It is concrete, NASA-sourced and visual. It also follows our best-performing Short pattern [H, see analytics snapshot]: a contradiction stated in the first line ("Its rocket could only throw it as far as Jupiter. It's now in interstellar space.").
- **Central scientific revelation:** **the planet never changes the spacecraft's speed, only its direction.** The speed gain exists only in the Sun's frame, because the planet is moving (C01 + C02). Everything else follows from this, including braking.
- **Best reference-frame demonstration:**
  - Play one computed flyby twice, back to back, with a **FrameSwitch**: (1) the camera rides with Jupiter, a symmetric hairpin, the readout "rel. Jupiter: 10 → 10"; (2) the camera holds still on the Sun, Jupiter slides across, and the same path stretches into a bent arc, with the readout now changing.
  - Then freeze and show the vector triangle: Sun-frame velocity = planet velocity + relative velocity, before and after the turn. The rotated relative arrow is the only thing that changed.
  - This replaces the train analogy.
- **Most compelling mission examples:**
  1. **Voyager 2**: the only four-planet tour, which also carries the 175-year alignment (C22, C25).
  2. **Parker Solar Probe**: the irony that the fastest spacecraft ever got there by being slowed down (C33).
  3. **BepiColombo**: braking used for capture, a concrete per-flyby number (−0.8 km/s, C37), and a live arrival on 21 Nov 2026.
  4. **Galileo at Io**: a supporting beat only.
- **Repetitive or over-complicated parts to cut:**
  - The three-chapter build-up to the frame reveal (merged).
  - The train analogy (demoted).
  - Angular-momentum terminology (say "orbital motion"; C08).
  - The Oberth effect, patched conics and the hyperbolic-excess formula: they are inside the animation's maths but never named.
  - Voyager 2's speed numbers (unverified).
  - The Grand Tour cancellation history: one line at most.

## 5. Production feasibility (2D Remotion)

**Fully achievable in 2D.** These are plane diagrams; the ecliptic view is the natural one. The physics can be computed in TypeScript, so the visuals are both accurate and deterministic (frame-driven, no timers):

- **Orbital trajectory animations:**
  - The planet-frame hyperbola comes from the hyperbolic excess speed and closest-approach distance (turn angle δ from sin(δ/2) = 1 / (1 + r_p·v∞²/μ)).
  - Heliocentric ellipses (Voyager's 1–5 AU counterfactual, Parker's shrinking orbits) come from perihelion and aphelion.
  - **New component:** `OrbitPath`.
- **Reference-frame transitions:**
  - The Sun-frame path = planet position(t) + relative path(t). Animate a blend factor from 0 to 1, so the hairpin visibly "unfolds" into the bent arc.
  - **New component:** `FrameSwitch` (or an extension of `CameraFrame`).
- **Velocity vectors:**
  - Tip-to-tail addition, rotation of the relative-velocity arrow, and a speed readout tied to arrow length.
  - **New component:** `VectorArrow`.
  - Arrows are cyan for the spacecraft and amber for the planet, and also differ by label and line style, so meaning is never carried by colour alone.
- **Planetary motion:** planets on circular orbits at true *relative* rates (NASA-PFS velocities) for the alignment chapter. Show the 175-year alignment as a time-lapse with a date counter (`ScaleIndicator` reused for years and AU).
- **Before-and-after comparisons:**
  - Dashed = "what would have happened" (the style rule: conceptual is dashed); solid = actual.
  - Uses: Voyager 2 with and without Jupiter; Parker's first orbit against its final orbit; BepiColombo's speed bar shrinking over nine flybys (`StepDiagram` reused).
- **Reuse:** `ScaleIndicator`, `StepDiagram`, `Callout`, `TextReveal`, `Captions`, `timing.ts`.
- **Cost traps:**
  - Don't draw trajectories to true scale where flyby distances are invisible. Use inset "zoom" circles for the flyby itself, against the solar-system scale, and say the scale change in narration (no disclaimer tags).
  - Don't hand-key paths. Compute them, so they can't be physically wrong.

**3D: not recommended for the core.** The one place it could help is Voyager 1 leaving the ecliptic plane after Titan (C21). An optional 3D tilt shot could show "up and out of the plane," but a 2D side-on inset does the same job. If a later episode needs 3D, Blender is better spent there.

## 6. Verdict

**Ready with specific corrections.** The physics is fully supported by primary NASA, ESA and NASA-data sources. No fundamental issues remain. Before scripting:

1. Adopt the 7-chapter structure (or at least merge chapters 1–3 and drop the train analogy as a chapter).
2. Apply the six corrections in fact-check §9: Voyager 1 vs 2 on the grand tour, the qualified "harder", "flybys plus ion engines", Jupiter's share, no Voyager 2 speed number, and "interstellar space" wording.
3. Treat BepiColombo's arrival (C38) and Voyager 1's light-day (C30) as future events. Re-check both after they happen.
4. Optional, for full primary sourcing: open the Flandro (1966) and Van Allen (2003) papers to replace the secondary attribution in C27, and to add a peer-reviewed quote for the energy exchange (C08 already stands on NASA).
