# Episode 003 — Competitor Evidence

Every number below was read from public YouTube search and channel pages on **2026-10-31**. The pages were loaded logged out in the built-in browser and parsed from the page's own data (`ytInitialData`). Nothing was logged in and no account was used.

**How to read this:**
- **Views and age** are YouTube's public labels. "6mo ago" is YouTube's rounding.
- **Channel median** is the median views of the channel's 30 most recent uploads on its `/videos` tab. It is skewed low for channels whose recent uploads are only days old.
- **Multiple** = video views ÷ channel median, following the `yt-viral` skill's method. It is an **estimate** (interpretation), not a YouTube metric.
- **Shorts** results carry no channel name in search data, so Shorts views can't be put against a channel baseline. They are evidence of format demand only.
- Search results are ranked by relevance, not by recency. "Recent median" is the median views of long-form results under 12 months old. A low recent median alongside many recent uploads signals **low-effort saturation**.
- No search-volume data was available, so search demand is unknown.

### The lesson for Episode 003
Channels like ours, small and animated, break out on **one impossible-sounding mechanism** (Second Order Science, IRA STUDIOS) or **a familiar thing seen inside** (Exploded Works, Simple Things). Generic topic titles don't break out. The same pattern showed up in our own data: SubmarineMessages, an invisible signal against a barrier, had the best hold on both Instagram and TikTok.
