| K01 | ASTM C1607-12(2025), *Standard Test Method for Determination of "Microwave Safe for Reheating" for Ceramicware* (store page; scope and significance) | ASTM International | Standards body | https://store.astm.org/c1607-12r25.html | Scope 1.1: "Most ceramicware is minimally absorbing of the microwave energy and will not heat up significantly." / "there are some products that absorb microwave energy to a greater extent and can become very hot in the microwave" and "pose a serious hazard". 5.4: "Dishes having a metallic glaze, decoration or paint should not be used in this test or microwave ovens" unless they are specifically designed for and marked as microwave safe. | K02 | *Microwaving food in plastic: Dangerous or not?* (20 Sep 2017) | Harvard Health Publishing | Medical school publication | https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthy-aging-and-longevity/microwaving-food-in-plastic-dangerous-or-not | "The FDA requires manufacturers to test these containers using tests that meet FDA standards and specifications." / "Only containers that pass this test can display a microwave-safe icon, the words "microwave safe,"…" / unlabelled containers "aren't necessarily unsafe; the FDA simply hasn't determined whether it is or not" / "Most takeout containers, water bottles, and plastic tubs or jars made to hold margarine, yogurt…" aren't microwave-safe / "Old, scratched, or cracked containers … may leach out more plasticizers." / "Check the label." |
| K03 | *Microwave Ovens: Dangers of Superheating* (29 Mar 2011) | Washington State Univ. CAHNRS Communications | University extension news | https://news.cahnrs.wsu.edu/article/microwave-ovens-dangers-of-superheating/ | "the water does not appear to be boiling"; "Superheating may occur when you heat water in a clean cup."; trigger: "even a slight movement like picking up the cup or adding hot chocolate mix or a spoonful of instant coffee"; "follow the recommendations in your microwave's instruction manual for heating times". |
| K04 | *Superheating* (LecDem blog, 23 Dec 2019) | Univ. of Maryland Physics, Lecture-Demonstration Facility | University physics outreach | https://lecdem.physics.umd.edu/tools-and-resources/lecdem-blog/357-superheating-blog.html | Bubbles "usually first form around nucleation sites, tiny (or not so tiny) impurities in the water"; with none, and no agitation, "no single tiny bubble manages to overcome the atmospheric pressure and surface tension to start the liquid boiling"; once disturbed, "suddenly there are lots of nucleation points, and the boiling begins in earnest"; the demo mug was "freshly cleaned, smooth". |
