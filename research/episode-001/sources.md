| S02 | Cooking with Microwave Ovens (last updated 2024-06-27) | USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service | Government, primary | https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/cooking-microwave-ovens | Magnetron converts wall power into short radio waves at ~2450 MHz; absorbed by water, fats, sugars; 1–1½ in penetration, centre heated by conduction; not inside-out; oven air stays at room temperature; uneven cooking and cold spots; turntable helps but stirring is still advised |

| S04 | Magnetron Operation | HyperPhysics, Georgia State University | University reference | https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Waves/magnetron.html | Hot cathode emits electrons (thermionic emission); axial magnetic field sweeps them around; energy transferred to oscillations at the cavities' natural frequency; radiation is the output |
| S05 | Why are the microwaves in a microwave oven tuned to water? (2014-10-15) | Christopher S. | S06 | Microwave Safety (peer reviewed, updated 2022) | Brenda Aufdenkamp, University of Nebraska–Lincoln Extension | University extension | https://food.unl.edu/article/microwave-safety/ | 1–1½ in penetration; turntables exist because cooking can be uneven; stir/rotate to eliminate cold spots |

| S08 | Magnetrons (updated 2022-04-11) | Chris Woodford, Explain that Stuff | Established educational reference, secondary | https://www.explainthatstuff.com/how-magnetrons-work.html | Plain-language magnetron description: heated cathode, curved electron paths, resonating cavities, waveguide into the cooking compartment |
